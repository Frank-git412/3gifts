# M5 context corrections after the first human review

Francesco supplied 39 unique ratings with constraint decisions and comments.
They cover the historical v113/v128 first block, with 38 main ballots and one
supplementary ballot. Shared ballots represent 24 v113 and 29 v128 main positions.
Scores of 4–5 occur on 13/24 and 12/29 positions respectively. These are calibration
observations, not an independent acceptance result for the following release.

## Findings

- 21 ballots report no violated constraint; 18 request verification (6 reading,
  9 technology, 3 skincare). Their comments concern suitability, not a specified
  hard-constraint violation. Their editorial review remains open.
- Replaying the technology listings also exposed a technical mismatch on six
  flagged ballots and two other ballots: a power bank matched the smartphone
  query, and a speaker matched the notebook stand query through "supporta" and
  feature text. Those unrelated queries bypassed connection requirements.
- Priority events changed ranking, but the pilot did not schedule a query from
  the reviewed event families. A generic result could fill the output first.
- A request for a relaxing gift became a mandatory product tag. This excluded
  otherwise eligible care routines and plausible leisure uses.
- The old frozen replay derived note signals but omitted the actual browser
  request serializer, including its recipient device context. Historical ratings
  and results are preserved. The new comparison checks this difference separately.

## Changes

Event-first contexts reserve a qualified query for each selected scope. These
queries respect exclusions, compatibility and explicit requested product types.
Three generic cards cannot stop the search before a scheduled event query.
The shared limit remains 12 queries and 75 seconds; no extra external service.

A requested relaxing mood is a soft preference. Explicit obligations remain
filters. Possible listening, photo viewing and care routines receive only partial
effect credit when the product text supports the use. This evidence is labelled
`editorial_possible_use_from_listing`: it is not a calming or clinical claim and
cannot satisfy a mandatory mood constraint. Generic novels and advertising words
no longer manufacture that mandatory evidence. The same mood is not scored twice
as both a note and an effect.

The iPhone model is recorded as context, without a taste bonus or inferred
connections. Actual compatibility checks remain mandatory. No ASIN, brand or
individual vote is hard-coded into production ranking.

Primary-object guards distinguish power banks, speakers and notebook stands.
Device mentions and a stem shared by "supporta" and "supporto" cannot turn those
products into a smartphone or stand. Correctly typed products remain eligible
when their required connection is explicitly provided and present in the listing.
The same replay exposed headphones entering through a microphone query. Primary
guards now cover headphones, microphones and phone stands as well. A recognized
connected product retains its recipient connection requirement even under a
secondary query; an intervening word in "microfono dinamico USB" cannot erase it.

## Validation and limits

`tests/m5-context-quality.mjs` checks bilingual requests and obligations, scope,
occasion retrieval, budgeted scheduling, limited mood credit, negative evidence,
compatibility, and the actual questionnaire payload. The existing G12/G13 suite
and assembled Worker flows remain part of the release gate.

One obsolete M3 assertion explicitly prohibited contextual queries for priority
events; it now verifies event precedence and preservation of the secondary effect.
Two UI checks now compare the returned revision with the canonical version constant.
The M3 reachability fixture now declares canonical connection identifiers and
their listing evidence; its old display labels were discarded by normalization.
The English headset positive cases remain covered alongside the rejection cases.

`scripts/m5-context-replay.mjs` first reproduces the historical v129 results, then
compares v129 and the candidate through each version's shipped request serializer.
Both engines receive the same frozen observations. Replays do not prove current
availability, live query retrieval, or human suitability. They are repeated for
determinism, with every candidate decision recorded.

`scripts/m5-live-context.mjs` provides a bounded post-publication check through the
public Worker with real Amazon responses. It uses credentials only in process
memory and stops at transport, quota, revision or invariant failures.

Acceptance remains open: fresh human ratings must reach 85% overall, 80% per
category and improve on baseline. The calibration sample cannot prove this.
Browser/mobile QA remains unavailable in this environment.

## Real-response follow-up

Six v130 requests returned 18 products using 16 Amazon calls and four cache hits.
Basic transport, budget, ASIN and affiliate checks passed, but manual review found
a printed poster matching a digital-frame query, two equivalent ebook readers,
and creams without a documented distinct use filling multiple slots. The follow-up
adds a digital-frame type guard, reader identity independent of book-like wording,
and conservative cream overlap. The observed responses are retained as regression
evidence. Occasion searches ran, but none of the nine anniversary proposals had
supported occasion evidence; editorial acceptance therefore remains open.
The v131 repeat exposed "lettore di libri elettrico" and the French "crème" as
additional spellings of those same function identities. They are now explicit
regressions. Neither spelling occurs in the frozen pool: the recorded v131 full
replay is retained, with a separate final-patch impact check rather than a claim
that another full replay was run.
